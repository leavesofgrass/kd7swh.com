Reactance

E5B · E5C

The arithmetic underneath half of E5. You compute a reactance first, then every other module uses it.

XL = 2πfL   ·   XC = 1 / (2πfC) f in hertz, L in henries, C in farads. µ = 10⁻⁶, n = 10⁻⁹, p = 10⁻¹².

Straight from the pool

E5C10 asks you to plot a 400-Ω resistor in series with 38 pF at 14 MHz — but first you must find XC.

Show the answer
XC = 1 / (2π × 14 × 10⁶ × 38 × 10⁻¹²) ≈ 299 Ω. The impedance is 400 − j300. (Continues in Rect ↔ Polar below.)

Traps

  • Picofarads at HF give hundreds of ohms — if you get milliohms or megohms, a prefix slipped.
  • Keep f in plain hertz inside the formula: 14 MHz = 14,000,000 Hz.

Resonant Frequency

E5A

Where XL = XC. Series resonance: impedance minimum, current maximum, voltage and current in phase. Parallel: impedance maximum.

fr = 1 / (2π√(LC)) Resistance is not in this formula — the exam lists R anyway, as bait.

Straight from the pool

E5A02 R = 22 Ω, L = 50 µH, C = 40 pF. Resonant frequency?

Show the answer
Ignore the 22 Ω. fr = 1/(2π√(50µ × 40p)) = 3.56 MHz.

E5A10 R = 33 Ω, L = 50 µH, C = 10 pF. Resonant frequency?

Show the answer
Quarter the capacitance → double the frequency: 7.12 MHz. (Same L as E5A02, C ÷ 4.)

Traps

  • R is a decoy in every resonance calculation on this exam.
  • Wrong answers sit at 2× and ½× the right one — dropping the 2π or the square root lands you exactly on a listed choice.
  • E5A10 offers 7.12 kHz and 7.12 MHz. Read the unit.

Q and Half-Power Bandwidth

E5A · E4B · E9D

Q measures how sharp a resonance is. Higher Q, narrower bandwidth — true for tuned circuits, matching networks, and antennas alike.

BW = fr / Q   ·   Qseries = X / R   ·   Qparallel = R / X Series and parallel Q are reciprocals — the exam asks the parallel form (E5A09).

Straight from the pool

E5A11 fr = 7.1 MHz, Q = 150. Half-power bandwidth?

Show the answer
7,100 kHz / 150 = 47.3 kHz.

E5A12 fr = 3.7 MHz, Q = 118. Half-power bandwidth?

Show the answer
3,700 kHz / 118 = 31.4 kHz.

Traps

  • Half of the correct value is always listed — do not halve the half-power bandwidth. The −3 dB points already define it.
  • Answers appear in both Hz and kHz. 47.3 kHz, not 157.8 Hz.
  • Antenna corollary (E9D06/E9D08): loading coils want high X/R for efficiency, but that raises Q and narrows SWR bandwidth. The exam tests both directions.

RC Time Constants

E5B

One time constant charges a capacitor to 63.2% of the applied voltage, or discharges it to 36.8%. Those two percentages are the only ones this pool asks.

τ = R × C Handy identity: megohms × microfarads = seconds, directly.

Straight from the pool

E5B04 Two 220 µF capacitors and two 1 MΩ resistors, all in parallel. Time constant?

Show the answer
Parallel capacitors add: 440 µF. Parallel resistors halve: 0.5 MΩ. τ = 0.5 × 440 = 220 seconds — right back at the number you started with, which is exactly why the decoys are 55, 110, and 440.

Traps

  • Caps add in parallel; resistors divide. Every wrong combination of those two rules lands on a listed decoy.
  • 63.2% charging and 36.8% discharging (E5B01) — the old multi-time-constant percentages are gone from this pool.

Phase Angle — ELI the ICE Man

E5B

Three pool questions, one formula, and a mnemonic: in an inductive circuit (ELI) voltage leads current; in a capacitive circuit (ICE) voltage lags.

θ = arctan( (XL − XC) / R ) Positive result → net inductive → voltage LEADS. Negative → net capacitive → voltage LAGS.

Straight from the pool

E5B07 XC = 500, R = 1,000, XL = 250. Phase relationship?

Show the answer
Net X = 250 − 500 = −250. θ = arctan(250/1000) = 14.0°, voltage lagging (net capacitive — ICE).

E5B11 XC = 25, R = 100, XL = 75. Phase relationship?

Show the answer
Net X = +50. θ = arctan(50/100) = 27°, voltage leading (net inductive — ELI).

Traps

  • Every answer set offers the angle and its complement, each in both directions — two separate ways to miss. 14° pairs with 68.2°, 27° with 63°.
  • Subtract XC from XL, not the reverse — the sign carries the lead/lag verdict.

Rectangular ↔ Polar (Figure E5-1)

E5C

Two-step problems: compute the reactance, then place R + jX on the complex plane. Resistance runs along the horizontal axis; +j (inductive) is up, −j (capacitive) is down.

Z = R ± jX   ·   |Z| = √(R² + X²)   ·   θ = arctan(X/R)

Straight from the pool

E5C10 400 Ω in series with 38 pF at 14 MHz → which point on Figure E5-1?

Show the answer
XC ≈ 299 Ω → Z = 400 − j300 → right of center, below the axis: Point 4.

E5C11 300 Ω in series with 18 µH at 3.505 MHz?

Show the answer
XL ≈ 396 Ω → Z = 300 + j400 → above the axis: Point 3.

Traps

  • The figure's points differ mostly by the sign of j — capacitive plots below the axis, inductive above.
  • Pure reactance sits on the vertical axis at ±90°; pure resistance on the horizontal (E5C03, E5C07).

Admittance and Susceptance

E5B

Admittance Y is the reciprocal of impedance, measured in siemens. Susceptance B is its imaginary part. Letters matter: G conductance, B susceptance, Y admittance.

Y = 1 / Z In polar form that means: take the reciprocal of the magnitude AND flip the sign of the angle. Two operations — the distractors do only one.

Traps

  • E5B03: reciprocal of magnitude and negate the angle — both, always.
  • E5B02: the letter for susceptance is B; conductance is G.

Real vs. Reactive Power

E5D

Only resistance dissipates power. Reactance stores energy in fields and hands it back — "wattless, nonproductive power" in the pool's own words.

P = I² × R R is the resistance alone — never |Z|, never R + X.

Straight from the pool

E5D11 100 Ω resistor in series with 100 Ω inductive reactance, 1 A flowing. Power consumed?

Show the answer
1² × 100 = 100 watts. The 141.4 W decoy uses |Z|; the 200 W decoy adds R + X. Only the resistor heats.

Inverting Op-Amp Gain

E7G

Four pool questions, one division. The circuit inverts — the output sign flips, and one question exists purely to catch that.

AV = −RF / R1 "Absolute voltage gain" means report the magnitude; a computed output voltage keeps the minus sign.

Straight from the pool

E7G09 R1 = 1,000 Ω, RF = 10,000 Ω, input +0.23 V DC. Output voltage?

Show the answer
Gain −10 × 0.23 V = −2.3 volts. Positive 2.3 V is the trap. It inverts.

E7G10 R1 = 1,800 Ω, RF = 68 kΩ. Absolute voltage gain?

Show the answer
68,000 / 1,800 = 38. Note the mixed units — kΩ over Ω.

Traps

  • RF arrives in kilohms while R1 arrives in ohms. Convert first.
  • Distractors sit at 2× and ½× the correct ratio.

Decibels, dBm, and the Link Budget

E4C · E4D

dBm turns multiplication into addition: 0 dBm is 1 milliwatt, +10 dB is ×10, +3 dB is ×2. A link budget is just a sum down the signal chain.

dBm = 10 log₁₀(P / 1 mW)  ·  RX level = TX + gains − losses  ·  ΔNF = 10 log₁₀(BW₂/BW₁)

Straight from the pool

E4D13 10 W transmitter (+40 dBm), 6 dBi and 3 dBi antennas, 100 dB path loss. Received level?

Show the answer
40 + 6 + 3 − 100 = −51 dBm.

E4D12 Same idea plus MDS −103 dBm and 6 dB required SNR. Link margin?

Show the answer
Received: 40 + 10 − 3 − 136 = −89 dBm. Needed: −103 + 6 = −97 dBm. Margin = −89 − (−97) = +8 dB. Add the SNR to the MDS before comparing.

E4C06 Receiver bandwidth widens from 50 Hz to 1,000 Hz. Noise floor rises by?

Show the answer
10 log₁₀(20) = 13 dB — think 10 dB (×10) + 3 dB (×2).

Traps

  • The dBm-to-watts ladder: −100 dBm = 0.1 picowatt (E4D14). Decoys are the neighboring powers of ten.
  • Memorize the mental pairs: 3 dB = ×2, 10 dB = ×10, so 13 dB = ×20 and 6 dB = ×4.

ERP and EIRP

E9A

Sum every loss the question lists, add the antenna gain, convert the net dB back to watts. Each pool question adds one more loss term than the last.

ERP = TPO × 10^(net dB / 10)   ·   dBi = dBd + 2.15 Gain given in dBd → ERP. Gain in dBi → EIRP. Same arithmetic, different reference antenna.

Straight from the pool

E9A02 150 W, 2 dB feed line loss, 2.2 dB duplexer loss, 7 dBd gain. ERP?

Show the answer
Net: 7 − 4.2 = +2.8 dB → 150 × 1.9 ≈ 286 W.

E9A12 An antenna has 6 dBi gain. Gain in dBd?

Show the answer
6 − 2.15 = 3.85 dBd. The dipole already has 2.15 dB over isotropic.

Traps

  • Miss one listed loss (duplexer, circulator) and you land on a decoy built for exactly that miss.
  • Mental anchors: +1 dB ≈ ×1.26, +2 dB ≈ ×1.6, +3 dB = ×2.

Feed Lines, Velocity Factor, and Stubs

E9F

Electrical length shrinks by the velocity factor. And the stub rules compress to three lines worth memorizing cold.

length (m) = fraction of λ × (300 / fMHz) × VF ¼-wave INVERTS its far end (short → open). ½-wave REPEATS it. ⅛-wave shorted = inductive, open = capacitive.

Straight from the pool

E9F06 Air-insulated parallel-conductor line, electrically ½ wavelength at 14.10 MHz. Physical length?

Show the answer
Air line VF ≈ 0.97–1. (300/14.10)/2 ≈ 10.6 meters. The 8.5 m decoy wrongly applies coax's VF of 0.66–0.80.

E9F09 Impedance of a ¼-wave line shorted at the far end?

Show the answer
Very high — the quarter wave inverts. (Open ¼-wave → very low; shorted ½-wave → very low, it repeats.)

Traps

  • Match the VF to the line type: air ≈ 1, foam higher than solid dielectric, solid coax ≈ 0.66.
  • Recite the stub table as pairs before the exam: ¼ inverts, ½ repeats, ⅛ shorted is an inductor, ⅛ open is a capacitor.

Impedance Matching — the Q-Section

E9E · E9G

A quarter-wave transformer's impedance is the geometric mean of the two impedances it joins. The Smith chart questions are pure vocabulary.

Zsection = √(Z₁ × Z₂) Smith chart: circles = constant resistance, arcs = constant reactance, the one straight line = the resistance axis.

Straight from the pool

E9E06 Match a 100-Ω feed point to 50-Ω line with a quarter-wave section of what impedance?

Show the answer
√5000 = 70.7 Ω, which isn't offered — pick the nearest standard line: 75 Ω. The 62-Ω decoy is the arithmetic average.

Forward, Reflected, and S-Parameters

E4B · E9E

The load absorbs what doesn't come back. And the S-parameter questions are a two-line vocabulary list.

Pabsorbed = Pforward − Preflected S11 = input return loss (the SWR-like one). S21 = forward gain. Subscripts: measured-at, driven-at.

Straight from the pool

E4B06 100 W forward, 25 W reflected. Power absorbed by the load?

Show the answer
100 − 25 = 75 watts. The decoys add them (125) or split the difference (112.5).

FM Modulation Index and Deviation Ratio

E8B

Same division, different inputs: modulation index uses the deviation and modulating frequency of the moment; deviation ratio uses the maximums.

m = deviation / modulating frequency   ·   DR = max deviation / highest modulating frequency

Straight from the pool

E8B05 Carrier swings ±5 kHz, highest modulating frequency 3 kHz. Deviation ratio?

Show the answer
5 / 3 = 1.67. "Plus or minus 5 kHz" means the one-sided deviation is 5 — doubling it is the trap.

E8B04 ±6 kHz deviation, 2 kHz modulating frequency. Index?

Show the answer
6 / 2 = 3.

Digital Signal Bandwidth

E8C

Two formulas and one memorized constant cover the whole group.

CW: BW ≈ 4 × WPM (Hz)  ·  FSK: BW = baud + 1.2 × shift  ·  FT8 = 50 Hz, always

Straight from the pool

E8C05 Bandwidth of 13-WPM International Morse?

Show the answer
≈ 4 × 13 = 52 Hz. The decoys use ×1, ×2, and ×8.

E8C07 4,800-Hz shift, 9,600-baud ASCII FM. Bandwidth?

Show the answer
9,600 + 1.2 × 4,800 = 15.36 kHz. Not Carson's rule — this exam wants baud + 1.2 × shift.

Bits, Levels, Nyquist, Dividers

E8A · E7F · E7A

Everything here is a power of two. n bits give 2ⁿ levels; n flip-flops divide by 2ⁿ; sampling needs 2× the highest frequency.

levels = 2ⁿ  ·  bits needed = ⌈log₂(range/resolution)⌉  ·  fsample ≥ 2 × fmax

Straight from the pool

E8A09 How many levels can an 8-bit A/D converter encode?

Show the answer
2⁸ = 256.

E7F06 Measure 1 volt at 1 millivolt resolution — minimum bits?

Show the answer
1,000 steps needed; 2⁸ = 256 is too few, 2¹⁰ = 1,024 → 10 bits.

E7A04 Divide a signal's frequency by 16 — how many flip-flops?

Show the answer
log₂16 = 4.

Band-Edge Arithmetic

E1A

The displayed carrier frequency is not where the signal ends. USB occupies the 3 kHz above the display; LSB the 3 kHz below. The whole signal must stay inside the segment.

USB: carrier … carrier + 3 kHz   ·   LSB: carrier − 3 kHz … carrier Edges to know cold: 20 m top = 14.350; 75 m Extra phone floor = 3.600; 20 m data/phone boundary = 14.150.

Straight from the pool

E1A01 3 kHz-wide USB with the display reading 14.348 MHz — legal?

Show the answer
No — the signal reaches 14.351, and 1 kHz of it sits above the 14.350 band edge.

E1A04 A station calls CQ at 3.601 MHz LSB. Legal for you to answer there?

Show the answer
No — your LSB sidebands would reach down to 3.598, below the 3.600 phone-segment floor.

Not on This Exam — Don't Study It

Older guides drill these. The 2024–2028 pool (verified against the February 2026 errata) removed them — spend the hours elsewhere.

  • Energy storage numerics (E = ½CV², E = ½LI²) — gone.
  • Power-factor multiplication problems — gone; PF survives only as a distractor word.
  • Transformer turns-ratio calculations — gone.
  • The old PEP-from-peak-to-peak-voltage problems (×1.414 conversions) — gone; only the 2.5:1 ratio remains.
  • Numeric radio-horizon distance formulas — gone; only "15% farther" remains.
